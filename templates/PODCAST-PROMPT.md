## Episode Prompt

> **No preamble — start the lesson immediately.** Do not open with exam logistics, domain
> weightings, question counts, price, validity, or study-strategy talk; the course overview
> episode already covered all of that and repeating it wastes minutes. No extended welcome or
> host banter. Within the first thirty seconds, state in one sentence what this module teaches,
> then dive straight into the first concept. If the source chunks open with exam-domain framing,
> skip past it. Exam domains and weights may be mentioned at most once, in passing, mid-episode —
> never as an opening. Do not recite exam-logistics numbers at any point.
>
> **Practitioner depth, not ELI5.** Assume the listener is a working engineer preparing for a
> professional certification — move fast and go deep. Use precise technical terminology
> throughout; at most ONE brief analogy per concept, then return to the mechanism itself. Speak
> the real numbers, dimensions, and formulas aloud rather than waving at them. Never re-explain a
> term after its first definition, and never pad with reassurance. A listener 20 minutes in
> should have learned a dozen precise concepts, not a few terms. Density and pace are the point
> of a deep dive.
>
> Produce an engaging, teaching-style two-host deep-dive episode on **<topic>** for a candidate
> preparing for the **<CERT>** certification exam. Cover the source's <N> chunks in order, giving
> each real time: (1) <chunk 1 and the specific things to hit>; (2) <chunk 2 — and if one example
> is the heart of the episode, say so: "walk that example out loud">; ... (N) <chunk N>.
>
> **Define on first use:** <term> · <term> · <term>.
>
> **Call out these exam traps explicitly:** <the precise misconception and its correction> ·
> <another>.
>
> **Do not drift into** <topic a later module owns> or <topic>.
>
> **Learning objectives:** the listener should be able to <verb specific thing>, <verb thing>,
> and recite <the one-liner worth memorizing>.
>
> End with three self-check questions: "<question>", "<question>", "<question>".
